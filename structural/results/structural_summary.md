The last run (uniform, n = 10 000) is an addition to the requested set. It
checks whether `hazard_min` falling below p on uniform is sampling noise (it
is; see item 5).

**Arms.** `classic`: heights drawn at insertion, never changed (control).
`gated-K1`, `gated-K3`: the self-referential rule of Definition 1.1 with
K = 1, 3. `exact`, `morris`: the paper's counter-based estimators
(`def:estimators`, Appendix A; `ExactEstimatorPolicy`,
`MorrisEstimatorPolicy`). `exact-legacy`, `morris-legacy`: the old RSASL and
RSASLA rules, verbatim (`ExactGatePolicy`, `MorrisGatePolicy`). **The legacy
arms do not measure any rule the paper defines or proves.** They are named
baselines, so a figure can say which rule it measured and a reader of the
ICALP submission can see how the old rules compare. The old exact rule loses
`H >= rank` only for p > 1/2 and the old Morris rule fails the crossing tail
(T1), so at p = 1/2 their numbers are not evidence about either defect. No
weight-floor arm is in these runs (the floor is cut from the submission);
`--floor=1` adds them for the full version.

**Workloads.** n keys inserted, then F accesses. Zipf s = 1.5 (the old
paper's workload), Zipf s = 1.0, uniform (the adversarial control), and 90/10
(90% of accesses to a random 10% of the keys, uniform within each part, as in
`setbench/README.md`). Every run draws a fresh random permutation of ranks to
keys, so key order and rank are uncorrelated. Within a run all arms see the
same access sequence.

**Accounting** (unchanged from the harness). Insertions are not costed. Each
access costs vertical span + horizontal steps as served, before promotion; a
successful search stops at the item's top level. Ent(sigma) = F + sum f_x
log_b(F/f_x) over the accesses. A = sum over accesses of
a_t = 1 + log_b((n + t)/(1 + f_x(t))), i.e. Theorem A's benchmark with the
n insertions counted as charges and as operations on their items.

**Columns.** `q99 cost_t/a_t`: 99th percentile over all accesses of a run of
the per-search ratio, from a histogram with 0.05 bins (upper bin edge, capped
at the run's max); `max cost_t/a_t`: largest per-search ratio in any run. KL
is KL(OPT || OBS) with OBS(x) proportional to b^(H_x). Depth = H_max - min_x
H_x. Gap = H_x - log_b(1 + f_x) over all items (Section 2's quantity, with j
the operations on x so far); "gap mean" is the mean over items (mean and sd
over runs), "gap min" and "gap max" the extremes over all items of all runs.
Compressed pointers follow `def:compression`: per node, the distinct non-null
successors among its forward pointers; "head incl." counts the head node too.
Bits per item, compressed = (compressed pointers, head incl.) x
(ceil(log2(n+1)) + ceil(log2(H_max+1))) + estimator bits, all over n; the
second factor is the span counter. Estimator bits are 0 for classic and gated,
sum of ceil(log2(Q_x+1)) for morris, sum of ceil(log2(f_x+1)) for exact, and
the same with R_x and f_x for the legacy arms. Bits per item, uncompressed =
sum of heights x ceil(log2(n+1)) / n, pointer bits only, no estimator.
`hazard_min` = min over levels l with at least 50 items at height >= l of
#{H >= l+1} / #{H >= l}. This is an across-items sanity view, **not** the
per-item fixed-time hazard of Corollary 3.5 (`cor:hazard`).

**Figures** (results/, drawn from the CSVs above): `structural_cost.pdf`
(cost/Ent against accesses, one panel per distribution, mean and sd band),
`structural_kl.pdf` (KL, log y, geometric mean and multiplicative sd band),
`structural_depth.pdf` (H_max and depth against accesses on log x with the
guide log_b t, one row per distribution), `structural_space.pdf` (bits per
item, compressed solid and uncompressed dashed, for classic, gated-K3, exact
and morris; Zipf 1.5 and uniform).

## Interpretation

1. Cost against the entropy benchmark is flat in F and in n. At t = F and
   n = 1000 every adaptive arm (the paper's four rules and the two legacy
   rules) is at 1.21 to 1.73 times Ent on all four distributions; at
   n = 10 000, 1.25 to 1.79. Run-to-run sd is at most 0.28. On Zipf 1.5 the
   paper's rules are at 1.28 to 1.41 at n = 1000 and 1.31 to 1.41 at
   n = 10 000. The classic list is at 3.60 times Ent at n = 1000 and 4.62 at
   n = 10 000 on Zipf 1.5, and 2.1 on Zipf 1.0 and 90/10. On uniform every
   arm, classic included, is at 1.62 to 1.79: there is nothing to adapt to,
   and the adaptive arms pay at most 0.1 times Ent more than classic.
2. Per search, cost_t / a_t has a short tail. For the adaptive arms the 99th
   percentile is 2.1 to 3.3, and the largest ratio over the 5 x 10^6 searches
   per arm and distribution is 3.5 to 5.9, with the same ranges at
   n = 10 000. The classic arm's 99th percentile on Zipf 1.5 is 8.2 at
   n = 1000 and 10.5 at n = 10 000: it pays log n, not a_t.
3. Heights follow log_b j. The mean gap H_x - log_b(1 + f_x) is 0.7 to 2.5
   for every adaptive arm, distribution and n. It is about 0.8 larger for
   K = 3 than for K = 1 (the mean jump is 2 instead of 1). The smallest gap
   over all items of all runs is -3.6; for the exact estimator it is -0.91,
   as `H >= rank` requires (the gap is then > -1). The largest gap is 11 to
   17, about log_2 n plus a constant: the geometric upper tail maximised over
   n items. H_max tracks log_b(n + F), with a mean offset of -0.9 to +2.7
   and a largest offset of +5.1 (gated-K3), consistent with
   `lem:maxheight`.
4. On the skewed workloads the shape is much closer to OPT than the classic
   list's (KL at t = F: 0.45 to 1.3 against 5.2 on Zipf 1.5, 1.0 to 1.8
   against 2.9 on Zipf 1.0). KL levels off after about 10^5 accesses instead
   of tending to 0. On uniform the adaptive arms' KL (1.17 to 1.87) is not
   below the classic list's (1.15), so the shape does not become flat.
   OBS weights items by b^(H_x), so KL is dominated by the tallest items,
   and the geometric upper tail of the height (item 3) keeps it away from 0.
5. On the two Zipf workloads `hazard_min` is at least p for every adaptive
   arm (mean over runs 0.52 to 0.62). On uniform it is 0.45 to 0.47 for every
   arm, and on 90/10 (uniform within each part) 0.47 to 0.58. The classic
   arm, whose hazard is exactly p at every level by construction, reads 0.45
   on every workload at n = 1000. The statistic is a minimum over levels, and the
   smallest qualifying level holds only 50 to 100 items. At n = 10 000 on
   uniform the classic arm still reads 0.46 and the adaptive arms 0.45 to
   0.49. So the shortfall is the selection bias of the minimum, the same for
   every arm, and not a failure of the hazard bound.
6. Space. The compressed pointer count is at most 1.78 n over every sample of
   every run (head included), against the 2n of `lem:pointercount`; classic
   is at 1.50 n. Compressed bits per item are 21 to 33 at n = 1000 and 27 to
   39 at n = 10 000. From t = 10^4 to t = F they grow by 0.1 to 4.9 bits for
   gated (more distinct pointers, and the span counter widening by one bit),
   2.4 to 5.3 for Morris, and 6.0 to 7.0 for exact, whose count bits grow as
   sum log f_x. Uncompressed pointer bits grow 1.8x to 2.7x over the same
   range, to 56 to 111 bits per item at n = 1000 and up to 156 at
   n = 10 000 on uniform. For the classic list compression does not pay:
   21.0 bits compressed against 20.1 uncompressed, since its heights average
   2 and the span counter costs more than the pointers it saves.
7. At p = 1/2 the legacy rules are comparable to the paper's rules on every
   measure here, and slightly cheaper on Zipf 1.5: legacy exact's cost/Ent is
   1.21 against 1.28 at n = 1000 and 1.25 against 1.37 at n = 10 000. The
   differences are within one run-to-run sd, and these runs do not test the
   regimes (p > 1/2, the crossing tail) where the legacy rules fail.

## Height gap tail (added 2026-10-24)

`--items=PATH` makes `build/structural` write one row per item at t = F
(arm, run, key, f_x, H_x, p) to PATH, and `plot_structural.py` then draws
`structural_gap.pdf`: the fraction of items, over all items of all runs,
whose gap H_x - log_b(1 + f_x) is at least g, on a log axis, against
`lem:heightupper`'s p^(g - K) with K = 1. Runs: the zipf1.5 and uniform
commands above with `--items=results/structural_items_<dist>.csv` added
(the CSVs are gitignored). Reading: for the four paper rules the fraction
falls by a factor p per level from g = 1 on, parallel to the guide and below
it for K = 1 and the two estimators; gated-K3 runs about one level to the
right. This figure replaces the KL curve in the submission's Section 9,
since no theorem predicts the KL curve (item 4 of the Interpretation) while
this is the quantity Section 2 bounds. The KL figure stays here.
