# Epochal reseeding: persistent vs reseeded

Generated by `build/reseed` (bench/reseed.cpp), 2026-10-03. p = 0.5 throughout.
Arms: gated = self-referential gate, K = 3 (K = 1 for the barrier);
exact / morris = the old RSASL / RSASLA counters, no floor; `+reseed` =
Definition "Epochal reseeding" of combined/main.tex.

Cost = search cost (vertical span + horizontal steps) + all rebuild work
(1 per node + 1 per level linked). Profiles a-c: n = 1000, F = 200 n accesses,
R = 20, insertions not costed (as in structural.cpp); keys get a random
permutation of the Zipf ranks per run. Drift: Zipf 1.5 whose key permutation
is redrawn every 50 n accesses. "local Ent" = sum over epochs of
Ent(epoch-local access frequencies) under the same (sequence-determined)
schedule. "horizontal/F" = horizontal steps only, no rebuild work (the
zip-tree / treap depth proxy). Barrier: insert n, access each once, y m
times, x m times; F = 2n + 2m, insertions costed; y = n/4, x = 3n/4;
H_y, H_x at the end of the y-block. Quantiles are nearest-rank. Nothing was
reduced from the requested sizes; the R = 2000 barrier rows are an extra run
(results/reseed_barrier_R2000.csv), about 4 minutes.

## Interpretation

1. Stationary profiles: reseeding costs a constant factor, 1.1x to 1.2x on uniform and 1.9x on Zipf 1.5 in cost/Ent. About 3.9 (K=3) or 2.9 (exact, Morris) units per access are rebuild work (n(1 + E[H_0]) per N ~ n charges); the rest is relearning, since hot items climb again from fresh heights every epoch (horizontal/F rises too, 2.3 -> 4.4 on Zipf 1.5). In exchange the run-to-run sd of cost/Ent drops 3x to 20x.
2. Drift (hot set moves every 50 n accesses) does not rescue reseeding: the persistent arms stay cheaper against both the global and the epoch-local entropy. Heights never decrease, but stale towers from earlier phases cost little; the drift here is slow compared with the 1024-charge epochs, so this does not test fast drift.
3. Barrier, persistent arm: heavy tail at a scale that does not shrink with n. sd of cost/F is about 1.0 at both n = 100 and n = 1000; P(x-block/m >= t) falls about 2x per unit of t (0.43, 0.14, 0.04, 0.009 at t = 4, 6, 8, 10) and is the same curve for both n, i.e. the predicted p^u law. Reseeded arm: sd 0.18 -> 0.05 from n = 100 to 1000 (about 1/sqrt(n), the CLT version of the n/F scale), and its max sits within 0.15 of its median.
4. At these sizes the reseeded arm's level is higher than almost all of the persistent tail: reseeded cost/F is about 7.2 at n = 1000, which the persistent arm exceeds in only 2 to 3 of 2000 runs (3 exceed 7, 2 exceed 8, max 9.9). The theory's gain is in the confidence scale, not the typical cost; the crossover in mean would need F log(1/delta) to beat the constant overhead, which happens only at very small delta here.
5. The barrier's heavy tail lives in the vertical part: persistent horizontal/F at n = 1000 is 0.51 / 0.52 / 0.60 / 1.01 (q50/q90/q99/max over 2000 runs) while total cost/F spans 2.25 to 9.9. So the tree-host proxy shows at most a mild tail, consistent with the barrier being a skip-list vertical-span effect (H_max - H_x), not a depth effect.

## Follow-up: ceiling and phases

Generated by `build/reseed`, 2026-10-03, p = 0.5.

**Clocked ceiling** (`CeilingConfig{enabled, c1}` in skiplist.hpp). At every
charge, Lambda = floor(log_b ops) + c1, where ops counts insertions and
accesses so far, this one included. Any height produced by an insertion or an
on_access promotion is clamped to min(h, max(Lambda, 1)). Floor raises are not
capped, and the ceiling aborts if combined with reseeding. Barrier arms:
`gated1+ceiling2` (c1 = 2) and `gated1+ceiling4` (c1 = 4), with the same
structure seeds as the other arms. Sizes are as requested: n = 100 with
R = 2000 and n = 1000 with R = 200. The persistent and reseeded rows reproduce
the earlier tables exactly. CSVs: results/reseed_barrier_ceiling_n100.csv and
results/reseed_barrier_ceiling_n1000.csv.

**Phases** (fast drift): insert n keys, then T phases, where phase k accesses
key (k mod n) exactly n times, so F = T n. Gated K = 3, persistent vs
reseeded, R = 10, insertions not costed. "search/F" excludes rebuild work and
"cost/F" includes it. T = n is the requested case. Smaller T values are added
to show the growth, and they match the coordinator's T = 4/16/64/256 check.
CSV: results/reseed_phases.csv.

### Interpretation

1. The ceiling removes the barrier's heavy tail and lowers the mean.
   - At n = 1000, x-block/m has max 5.35 (c1 = 2) and 7.35 (c1 = 4), against
     10.86 for the persistent arm. P(x-block/m >= t) drops below 0.5% by
     t = 5 for c1 = 2 and by t = 7 for c1 = 4.
   - Mean cost/F is 1.82 (c1 = 2) and 2.18 (c1 = 4), below the persistent
     2.44 and far below the reseeded 7.2. So the prediction holds, and the
     mean comes out better than "near persistent".
   - The tail is cut at about c1 + 3 rather than having an n-independent
     exponential scale. For c1 = 2 it looks the same at n = 100 and
     n = 1000. Horizontal/F is unchanged or slightly lower.
2. The table's "mean H_y - H_x" column barely moves under the ceiling, because
   at the end of the y-block x has been accessed only once. That column is
   not the right view of the ceiling. The cap acts on H_y relative to log_b
   ops, which is what x catches up to during the x-block.
3. Phases: the persistent cost per access grows like log_b T.
   - At n = 1024, cost/F is 5.0, 7.9, 12.1, 15.5 and 19.4 for T = 4, 16, 64,
     256 and 1024, about 1.8 x global Ent/F.
   - Persistent vertical/F at n = 256 is 4.1, 5.2, 6.9 and 9.6, in line with
     the coordinator's 4.0/5.5/7.2/9.2.
   - Reseeded vertical/F is flat at 4.2 to 4.6. Its total is about 9 to 10:
     search about 4.5 to 6.2 plus about 4 of rebuild work, roughly 5 x the
     local Ent/F. Global Ent/F = 1 + log2 T exactly. Local Ent/F goes
     1.75 -> 2.0.
4. Crossover: reseeding is cheaper in total from T = 64 on at both n. At T = n
   it is cheaper by 1.5x (n = 256) and 1.9x (n = 1024). For T <= 16 the
   rebuild work (about 4 per access) still dominates.
5. Unlike the slow-drift profile, stale towers do cost here. Persistent
   horizontal/F grows from 0.8 to 7.6 as T grows, because every former hot key
   keeps a tall tower. The reseeded arm's horizontal/F also grows, 0.3 -> 1.8.
   That growth is mostly where the hot key sits: for small T the hot keys are
   the leftmost keys, while for T = n they sweep the whole list.

## Follow-up: ceiling with top-up

Generated by `build/reseed`, 2026-10-04, p = 0.5, K = 1.

`CeilingConfig{enabled, c1, topup}`. When Lambda rises from l-1 to l, one
sweep of the chain at level index l-2 (every node of height >= l-1) raises
each node of height exactly l-1 to l with probability p. The sweep costs 1
per node visited plus 1 per splice (`topup_work()`). The first tick sets
Lambda and does not count as a rise.

Arms: ceiling2 and ceiling4, with and without the top-up. All four arms use
the same structure seeds, but the top-up arms consume extra coin flips, so
their random streams diverge after the first rise. The no-top-up rows
reproduce the previous follow-up exactly. Sizes are as requested. CSVs:
results/reseed_barrier_topup_n100.csv and
results/reseed_barrier_topup_n1000.csv. Q = items at height exactly Lambda,
measured at the end of the run.

### Interpretation

1. The top-up work is negligible, as predicted: at most 55 units per run,
   1e-5 to 8e-4 per access.
2. Q stays within the bound. Mean Q at the end is 0.43 / 0.70 at n = 1000 for
   c1 = 2 without / with the top-up, against the bound 2. For c1 = 4 it is
   0.12 / 0.23, against the bound 2 b^(K+1-c1) = 0.5. Max Q is 2 in every
   arm. The barrier has only two hot items, so it is not a stress test for Q.
3. The prediction that nothing changes on the barrier does not hold. The
   top-up raises mean cost/F by about 0.25 (c1 = 2: 1.82 -> 2.07 at
   n = 1000, 2.02 -> 2.29 at n = 100), and shifts x-block/m by about +0.5.
   The 2000-run difference is about 20 standard errors.
4. The mechanism: y is a stale capped item once the x-block starts. Lambda
   rises exactly once during the x-block at both n (floor(log2 ops) goes
   13 -> 14 at n = 100 and 19 -> 20 at n = 1000). With the top-up, y follows
   the cap up with probability p at each rise.
   - The share of runs with y at the cap at the end of the y-block goes
     0.38 -> 0.56 at n = 1000 and 0.12 -> 0.35 at n = 100.
   - Mean final H_max rises by 0.2 to 0.5.
   - x never reaches y's height within m accesses, so every later x-search
     pays the extra level.
5. The effect is a bounded shift, not a tail. The maximum x-block/m moves by
   about +0.5 (c1 = 2) or +0.5 to +0.8 (c1 = 4), the tail still ends by
   t = 7 (c1 = 2) or t = 10 (c1 = 4), and no heavy tail returns.

## Follow-up: shadow (double-buffered) rebuild

Generated by `build/reseed --only=shadow --runs=20`, 2026-10-19, p = 0.5,
gated K = 3. CSV: results/reseed_shadow.csv.

**Shadow rebuild** (`ReseedConfig{true, true}` in skiplist.hpp; the design is
analysis/frontier.md "a shadow rebuild" / frontier.tex lead:shadowreseed; the
paper itself has no shadow variant). Same epoch schedule as Definition
"Epochal reseeding". At the first charge of each epoch an inactive second
list is started. After every charge the next ceil(d/N) + 1 keys of the active
list (2 here), in key order, are copied into it with fresh insertion-law
heights and policy state. Searches and promotions use only the active list.
At the boundary the shadow becomes active in O(1), and the old nodes are
recycled into the next shadow. Shadow work counts the same unit as rebuild
work: 1 per node plus 1 per level linked. In the dynamic case, a new key
behind the copy cursor makes the shadow stale and the boundary falls back to
the eager rebuild. A shadow cut short by a doubling is finished by the
boundary operation (a catch-up). Neither happens on a fixed set: both
counters are 0 in every run below.

`max_op_work` = the largest work of any single operation: its search cost
plus the rebuild and shadow work it performed. It is taken over all
operations, insertions included. The CSV also has `max_access_work`, the
same maximum over accesses only, which matches to within 1 on average.
Workloads and structure seeds are those of the stationary and phases
profiles above: the persistent and eager rows reproduce reseed_uniform.csv,
reseed_zipf1.0.csv, reseed_zipf1.5.csv and the n = 1024, T = 1024 runs 0-9
of reseed_phases.csv exactly. Phases now uses R = 20, not 10. Zipf 1.0 was
added for the stationary figure.

### Interpretation

1. The prediction holds. The shadow arm matches the eager arm in cost
   (cost/Ent within 0.4% on every profile, all inside one standard error)
   and in concentration (sd 0.007 vs 0.010, 0.022 vs 0.021, 0.078 vs 0.068,
   0.071 vs 0.086).
2. The worst single operation drops from about 4.2k for the eager arm, which
   is the full rebuild n(1 + E[H_0]) ~ 4n plus one search, to 49 to 55 on
   average and at most 59. That is about 1.3x the persistent arm's worst
   search (37 to 40). The shadow part of one operation is at most 29 to 35:
   two node copies, each costing 1 + an insertion-law height.
3. Total reorganization work per access is 3.94 vs 3.92 (stationary) and
   4.008 vs 4.004 (phases). The difference, n(1 + E[H_0])/F, is the last
   epoch's shadow, which is built but never swapped in.
4. The per-operation bound is O((1 + n/N) log n) as a maximum over a run, not
   O(log n + n/N). Each copied key costs 1 + H with H an insertion-law height,
   so the n/N term carries the maximum height. In expectation per operation
   it is O(1 + n/N).
